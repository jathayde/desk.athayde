# Phase 2.5 plan: the allocation module (draft for review)

Source of truth: `docs/artifacts/investment-copilot-prd.md` §5.12 and §6. Phase 2 (`docs/phase-2-plan.md`) is built and reviewed; its conventions hold (secrets in credentials, CUSIP identity, every "logged" action an `audit_events` row, no live Schwab calls in tests, unattended runs read-only, shadow mode until the operator turns it off, `bin/ci` green per step, DHH and counselors review per step). Nothing here auto-executes: allocation rules judge the whole book and feed the same proposal → approval pipeline; the approve gesture stays the only path to Schwab. Revised after the counselors plan reviews (codex-5.5-high, claude-fable); the user's answers to §8 come before step 1.

## 0. Decisions and assumptions

1. **Allocation policy is rule data in the existing editor, structurally separate.** `risk_rules` gains `scope` (`trade`, `book`), the one-active index becomes `(scope, rule_type, setup_id)`, and `RiskRule::TRADE_TYPES` and `BOOK_TYPES` are distinct lists; `Guardrails::Evaluator` queries `scope: :trade` only, the allocation jobs `scope: :book` only, and `/risk_rules` lists both under their own headings with the same supersede, version, preview, and halt-lock behaviour. Book rule types: `rebalance_band` (default 5 percentage points, overridable per target), `min_rebalance_notional` (default $100), `correlation_threshold` (default 0.6, window 60 trading days), `cash_drag_weeks` (default 3). **Deployment policy stays a table** as PRD §5.12 names it: `deployment_policies` (`mode` in `immediate`, `staged`, `ask`; `weeks`; `active`; `version`; `superseded_by_id`; `changed_by_id`; `note`), edited in the rule editor by superseding exactly like a rule, so the PRD's data model holds and the versioning is the same code path.
2. **Targets are data, edited in place with an audit row; bands are rules.** `allocation_targets` (`instrument_id` or `asset_class`, `target_weight`, `active`) is not a judge of any proposal, so it is not versioned; each change is `allocation_target_changed` with before and after. The drift band is the `rebalance_band` rule, versioned like every other rule (PRD §5.12: what becomes policy is versioned); there is no per-target override in Phase 2.5, and the effective band per target is the wider of the rule's band and one share of the instrument as a share of equity (§0.6), computed at drift time and shown on the targets screen. A rebalance proposal freezes the target, the band, and the weights it saw, as Phase 2 froze sizing.
3. **Tags and classes per the PRD.** `instrument_tags` (`instrument_id`, `tag` in `leveraged`, `amplified`, `diversifier`), unique per pair, edited on the instrument page with an `instrument_tagged` audit row; `leveraged` and `amplified` are excluded from mechanical proposals identically. `instruments.asset_class` (authored by hand, audited as `instrument_classified`) is how a class target finds its members; an instrument with both its own target and a class target is judged by its own and excluded from the class sum, and the targets screen refuses a set of active targets whose weights exceed 100 percent.
4. **Weights come from the desk's own numbers, and only fresh ones.** Weight = position market value ÷ the latest fresh balance's equity, cash = settled + unsettled ÷ equity. A drift run requires a fresh balance, a fresh positions feed, and a priced, non-delisted market value for every instrument any active target covers; otherwise it records `unavailable` with the stale, unpriced, or delisted components named, proposes nothing, and the dashboard says which target to retire or replace. An instrument with no class and no target is "unallocated" and shown, never proposed against.
5. **Rebalance orders are a new proposal kind, judged by what they do.** `OrderProposal.kind` gains `rebalance`; origin gains `allocation`; `Proposals.draft` and `Proposals::Create` accept `instrument:` so the jobs pass the target's instrument row and never re-resolve a symbol (CUSIP identity holds). The rules stop branching on `entry?` and `exit?` alone: `opens_exposure?` (entries and rebalance buys), `reduces_position?` (exits and rebalance sells), `requires_bracket?` (entries only). A rebalance carries an explicit quantity and no stop: quantity = drift notional ÷ the close the drift row used, whole shares, rounded down (fractional shares stay unverified); the limit price is that close, frozen on the drift row and shown in the Drift panel, and not editable (§8, step 2: reject and propose by hand to trade at another price), so `stop_collar` and `max_risk_per_trade` skip it; `position_coverage`, `settled_cash`, `notional_cap`, `wash_sale`, `max_positions`, `max_position_exposure`, and both halts judge it by those predicates; a rebalance sell takes the exit's child-action and tax-lot path; the payload is a plain LIMIT (`Schwab::OrderPayload.limit`, no bracket). Whether a rebalance buy should instead carry a bracket stop is a question for the user (§8).
6. **One planner, one intent per instrument and side, whole shares before the floor.** `Allocation::Planner` is the only thing that turns drift, an immediate inflow, or a due tranche into proposals: it reads every open allocation proposal, pending tranche, today's drift rows, and any rebalance the human rejected on the same target since the drift last crossed the band as committed intent, so the three jobs cannot propose against the same cash twice and a rejection is not re-proposed every night at a new price (such a target records `action: rejected_standing` until the human clears it on the targets screen or the drift changes sign). On a small book whole shares bind before dollars do: the planner computes the whole-share quantity first and proposes only if it is at least one share and the post-trade weight is nearer the target than now; anything else is `reported` with `detail: sub_share`, the `min_rebalance_notional` rule is a floor on the rounded order's value, never on the drift, and a tranche below one share of its target rolls into the next tranche instead of proposing. Each proposal it creates carries `allocation_source_type` and `allocation_source_id` (the drift row, the inflow, or the tranche), unique per `(account, instrument, side, source)`. Before proposing on an instrument it takes the account-and-instrument advisory lock `Proposals::Create` will use, and skips with `action: existing_proposal` if any pending or unresolved proposal exists there, so a human proposal and the job never cross. The nightly check writes one `allocation_drifts` row per target per day (weight, target, band, close, drift, notional, action in `none`, `reported`, `proposed`, `thesis_review`, `existing_proposal`, `rejected_standing`, `unavailable`), audited `allocation_drift_recorded`; the dashboard and the Sunday review read those rows, never recompute.
7. **Leveraged and amplified instruments never get a mechanical proposal.** Drift on a tagged instrument creates a `thesis_reviews` row (instrument, drift, the original thesis copied from the open trade or the watchlist item, current numbers, `decision` in `deploy`, `hold`, `exit`, `decided_at`, `decided_by`), audited `thesis_review_created` and `thesis_review_decided`. It shows in the proposals queue; `deploy` and `exit` open the manual proposal form prefilled, `hold` closes it with a reason. A thesis review with no thesis on file says so and still asks.
8. **Cash inflows come from the activities feed.** A deposit activity (Schwab transaction type unverified: recorded in `docs/verify-on-approval.md`) creates one `cash_inflows` row (activity, amount, detected_at, the policy version applied, status), audited `cash_inflow_detected` and `cash_inflow_status_changed`. Policy `immediate` hands the amount to the planner that night; `staged` writes a `deployment_tranches` schedule (N weekly tranches, audited `deployment_tranche_scheduled`, each handed to the planner on its date, audited `deployment_tranche_proposed`); `ask` creates a thesis review with reason `deployment_ask`. The base-rate context (lump-sum vs staged) is static text authored in the memory repo, frozen into the proposal's `context` at creation and shown as a "Deployment context" panel on the Flow 2 detail, never computed.
9. **Diagnostics are stored rows the Sunday review reads.** Cash drag: `Allocation::DiagnosticsJob` (Saturday) writes one `cash_drag_diagnostics` row (policy version, cash percent, weeks in cash from the snapshot series, threshold, flagged), audited `cash_drag_checked`. Correlation stress: the same job computes rolling 60-day daily-return correlations of each holding and each `diversifier` against SPY from Schwab price history (through `StopCollar::Candles`' client and rate-limit handling), stored in `correlations` (instrument, as_of, window, value) with SPY as the benchmark series only, never a row of its own, audited `correlation_checked`; the review flags diversifiers above the threshold. Fund flows: a `fund_flows` row per week (as_of, amount, source, fetched_at), entered on the system page by hand and audited `fund_flow_changed` until a fetch is verified; FMP is not configured, and ICI's published release has no verified stable URL (§8).
10. **Unattended runs stay broker-write-free and model-free.** The drift check, the diagnostics, the inflow job, and the tranche job write audited deterministic rows and create proposals through `Proposals::Create` with `origin: :allocation`, in shadow like every other proposal; they never call a model and never call a Schwab order endpoint. The Sunday review prompt reads the diagnostic rows as data.

## 1. Data model

- `risk_rules.scope` (`trade` default, `book`); the one-active index becomes `(scope, rule_type, setup_id)`.
- `deployment_policies`: `mode`, `weeks`, `active`, `version`, `superseded_by_id`, `changed_by_id`, `note`; one active row (partial unique index).
- `instruments.asset_class` (nullable string, authored).
- `allocation_targets`: `instrument_id` (nullable), `asset_class` (nullable, one of the two), `target_weight` decimal, `active`, timestamps; check constraint: exactly one of instrument or class.
- `instrument_tags`: `instrument_id`, `tag`, unique `(instrument_id, tag)`.
- `allocation_drifts`: `allocation_target_id`, `as_of` date, `weight`, `target_weight`, `band`, `close`, `drift`, `notional`, `quantity`, `action`, `detail` jsonb (the stale or unpriced components, or `sub_share`), `order_proposal_id` (nullable), `thesis_review_id` (nullable); unique `(allocation_target_id, as_of)`.
- `thesis_reviews`: `instrument_id`, `reason` (`drift`, `deployment_ask`), `thesis`, `context` jsonb (drift, weight, price, P&L), `decision`, `decision_reason`, `decided_at`, `decided_by_id`.
- `cash_inflows`: `activity_id` unique, `amount`, `detected_at`, `deployment_policy_id`, `status` (`pending`, `proposed`, `staged`, `asked`, `done`).
- `deployment_tranches`: `cash_inflow_id`, `due_on`, `amount`, `order_proposal_id` (nullable), `status`.
- `cash_drag_diagnostics`: `as_of`, `deployment_policy_id`, `cash_percent`, `weeks_in_cash`, `threshold_weeks`, `flagged`; unique `as_of`.
- `correlations`: `instrument_id`, `as_of`, `window_days`, `value`; unique `(instrument_id, as_of, window_days)`.
- `fund_flows`: `as_of` unique, `amount`, `source`, `fetched_at`.
- `order_proposals.kind` adds `rebalance`; `origin` adds `allocation`; `allocation_source_type` and `allocation_source_id` (nullable, unique with account, instrument, and side when present); `context` jsonb for the deployment panel.

## 2. Jobs and cadence (`config/recurring.yml`, America/New_York, trading days unless noted)

- `Allocation::DriftJob` 17:10 (built in step 1; 16:40 would often read positions synced before the close, since the off-hours cadence is an hour): weights, drift rows, then the planner (rebalance proposals or thesis reviews), one `allocation_drift_checked` audit per run with the counts. The run is `unavailable` unless the balance and the positions sync both postdate that day's regular close. A targeted instrument that is held is priced by its position's last price, so the close and the market value behind its weight agree; one that is not held is priced by its quote's last print, fetched once per run, and a failed fetch makes the whole run unavailable with that reason.
- `Allocation::InflowJob` after each activities sync (enqueued by `Sync::ActivitiesJob` like the journal linker): new deposits → inflow rows and the policy's first action through the planner.
- ~~`Allocation::TrancheJob` 09:00 on the tranche's due date~~: built into the 17:10 check instead (§8, step 4).
- `Allocation::DiagnosticsJob` Saturday 08:00: correlations for every held instrument and every diversifier against SPY, and the cash-drag row.
- Sunday review reads `allocation_drifts`, `correlations`, `cash_drag_diagnostics`, and `fund_flows`; nothing is computed in the prompt.

## 3. Screens

**Built in step 1, where it differs from below:** there is no instrument page, so the asset class and tags are edited on the targets screen (`/allocation`, "Classes and tags", through `instruments/:id/classification`) for every held, targeted, or watched instrument. Every write on the targets screen, the classification, and the deployment policy is the approver's, like the rule editor; the second user reads. A target names an instrument the desk already knows (as a proposal does): nothing on the screen asks Schwab.


- Dashboard: an "Allocation" section below positions, one line per target (weight vs target, drift, band, last action), unallocated holdings named; reads the latest drift rows.
- Rule editor: book rules listed under their own heading with the same forms; the preview for `rebalance_band` shows which targets would propose tonight.
- Targets: `/allocation` (desktop) to add, weight, and retire targets (refusing a set past 100 percent), showing each target's effective band and any standing rejection or hold to clear ("Ask again"); the asset class and tags are edited there too (see the step 1 note above); the deployment policy sits in the rule editor's book section.
- Proposals: a rebalance uses the Flow 2 detail with a "Drift" panel (weight, target, band, notional) in place of sizing and collar, and a deployment proposal adds the "Deployment context" panel frozen at creation; a thesis review has its own detail with the thesis panel and the three decisions.
- System page: fund-flow entry, and the last drift and correlation runs in the feeds table.
- Sunday review email: cash drag, correlation flags, fund flow, next to the edge stats.

## 4. Guardrail changes

`Guardrails::Rule` gains `opens_exposure?`, `reduces_position?`, and `requires_bracket?`, and every rule that branched on `entry?` or `exit?` is re-read against them: `stop_collar` and `max_risk_per_trade` judge only what requires a bracket; `position_coverage`, `wash_sale`'s sell branch, the child action, and the tax lots apply to anything that reduces a position; `settled_cash`, `notional_cap`, `max_positions`, `max_position_exposure`, `pending_new_positions`, and the entry halt apply to anything that opens exposure. Book rules never appear in `guardrail_results`.

**Built in step 2:** `earnings_blackout` and `max_sector_exposure` stay on entries only; on a rebalance they would only add a "not checked" acknowledgement to every order. `Proposals::Create` drafts outside the lock (an entry's draft reads Schwab) and then checks and writes under a transaction-scoped advisory lock on (account, instrument), the same lock the planner takes before its any-side check, so a human proposal and the planner cannot both pass. An allocation proposal is audited with the system as its actor and alerts like an agent's.

## 8. For the user to confirm (recorded, not blocking the build)

**Seeded default (step 1):** the deployment policy starts at `ask`, the one mode under which a deposit causes no mechanical action.

**Step 2 decisions, recorded for the user:**
- A rebalance proposal created at 17:10 expires at the next session's regular close, not at midnight, so it is there to decide in the morning.
- The limit is the close the drift row used and is not editable: no proposal has an edit path. To trade at another price, reject it and propose by hand. The rejection then stands on that target (`rejected_standing`) until the drift returns inside the band, changes side, or the operator presses "Ask again" on the targets screen.
- Class targets and leveraged or amplified instruments stay `reported` in step 2: a class has no instrument to order, and tagged instruments wait for step 3's thesis review.
- **Consequence of the no-stop answer, for the user to weigh:** a rebalance buy that opens a new position lands in the journal's review queue (no setup, no stop), and its position reads unprotected on the dashboard (the fourth banner) until a stop is placed by hand; the fill alert says so, though the hard unprotected alert (`Orders::ProtectionWatch`) watches entries only. A rebalance fill on an existing trade joins it without replacing its setup or stop. The Phase 2 protection model and the allocation module disagree here by design; a core-holding marker that exempts a position from the protection banner would be a later decision.
- The planner steps aside while anything on the instrument is waiting or approved and not yet filled or cancelled, a shadow approval awaiting hand placement included, so an unplaced rebalance is not proposed again the next night.
- A rebalance the rules block is written to its drift row as `reported` with the blocking rules named, the rejected proposal kept on the row. It is tried again the next night, since settled cash and the caps change; each attempt is one `rejected_by_rules` row and one alert.
- Settled cash counts proposals at the broker, as in Phase 2 (§0.9), so two rebalance buys proposed the same night each pass on their own; approving the first makes the second's re-evaluation see it, so nothing is over-committed, but the second may then be refused at approval.

**Step 3 decisions, recorded for the user:**
- One open thesis review per instrument: a later night's drift on it refreshes the numbers on the review already waiting rather than opening another.
- A hold stands like a rejected rebalance: not asked again while the drift stays outside the band on the same side, lifted by the drift coming back or changing side, or by "Ask again" on the targets screen.
- Deploy and exit record the decision and open the manual proposal form prefilled with the symbol and the thesis; the proposal written there is the operator's and goes through every guardrail. Deploying is an entry with a stop, sized by the risk rule, not a stop-less rebalance.
- A new review alerts by email and push, like an agent's proposal.
- After deploy or exit, the planner steps aside while the proposal the operator wrote is waiting or unfilled; if the operator walks away from it (rejected, expired, never written) the next night's drift asks again. Only a hold stands without a proposal.
- A review left open after the tag comes off is still answered before anything mechanical happens on that instrument, and a hold keeps standing whether or not the instrument is still tagged. Holds belong to the target whose drift raised them: a retired and re-added target starts fresh.
- A decision posted from a page whose numbers the nightly refresh has since replaced is refused, so a hold always stands on what the operator saw.

**Step 4 answers (2026-10-16) and what was built on them:**
- New cash is split pro-rata to the active instrument targets' weights; classes and leveraged or amplified instruments are left out, each share buys whole shares at that night's measured close as a stop-less rebalance buy, a share under the minimum or beside a waiting proposal is skipped, and what buys nothing stays cash.
- While a deposit is pending, asked, or has tranches still scheduled, the nightly check still proposes sells but records buys as `reported` ("buys wait while new cash is deployed"): one writer for buys at a time. The dashboard names the deployment in progress.
- "Ask each time" raises a thesis review about the deposit (no instrument): deploy tonight, stage over N weeks (the policy's weeks, else 4, editable on the review), or hold as cash with a reason.
- The Deployment context panel reads `deployment.md` from the memory repo, frozen into each deployment proposal; the panel says when the file does not exist yet.
- Built, not asked: only deposits on or after the day the desk first had a deployment policy count, so a backfill never deploys history; a deposit under `min_rebalance_notional` is recorded as done and left as cash (this also keeps a cash-in-lieu print, verify item 45, from deploying).
- A night either settles new cash or defers it. Buying, or a share too small to buy, settles it; unsettled cash, a halt, a proposal already waiting, a target with no price, a missing minimum rule, or an error defers it to the next night, up to five nights, after which a deposit is left as cash and a tranche rolls. Once any target takes a share the deposit or tranche is settled: the shares that deferred that night stay cash, since proposing them again would double the rest.
- The planner spends only settled cash not already claimed, tonight's earlier buys included, so an ACH deposit waits until it settles rather than proposing buys the rules would block (and alerting) each night.
- One tranche per deposit a night, the earliest due, read under its lock; tranches staged from an answered ask start the day of the answer. Weeks are 1 to 52.
- Only valid deposits with a positive amount count, and each is re-read before deploying: one returned, cancelled, or re-signed since detection is done, marked reversed. A deposit detected while a check is running waits for the next night.
- **Deviation from §2:** there is no 09:00 `TrancheJob`. Tranches due on or before a trading day are proposed by that evening's 17:10 check, priced from its fresh rows and expiring at the next session's close, like every other allocation proposal. A night whose check is unavailable deploys nothing and tries again the next night.

**Step 5, built:**
- Cash drag compares cash (settled plus unsettled over equity, from each week's latest balance) with the cash the active targets leave uninvested (one minus their weights), and counts back the consecutive weeks the excess stays above the rebalance band; flagged at the `cash_drag_weeks` rule. The snapshot series carries no cash, so balances are the source; targets are not versioned, so past weeks are judged against today's targets.
- Correlations use daily closes from Schwab's one-year history (the same cache the collar reads) for every held instrument and every diversifier, SPY as the benchmark only; a symbol with stale or short history is named in the audit row, and a rate limit ends the run with what was computed.
- Fund flows are entered on the system page in billions, one row per week, a second entry for the same week replacing the first with the before on its audit row.
- After review: correlations are measured over shared closes and refused, with the reason named, when a series stops before SPY's, has a gap longer than five days, moves more than 35% in a day (a possible unadjusted split; verify item added), or has no variance; the row carries its last close. A rate limit names every symbol it did not reach. Weeks and windows are whole numbers (1-52 weeks, a 3-200 day window). Cash drag counts from the latest week with a balance and records why the count stopped. The email marks rows older than a week as last checked or last measured, never flags from them, and names what the latest run could not measure. Fund flows are one row per week (the week's Monday), never future-dated.
- The Sunday email gains an Allocation section from stored rows: each target's latest drift, the cash-drag row, diversifiers above the correlation threshold, every correlation, and the latest fund flow. The system page shows the last drift and correlation runs and the fund-flow form.

**Phase-level review (DHH, counselors claude-opus and claude-sonnet), answered and fixed 2026-10-16:**
- New cash is held to the same answers as drift: a target with a standing hold or rejection, an open review, a class, or a leveraged or amplified instrument keeps its share as cash; a rejected deployment buy stands like a rejected rebalance.
- A deposit is split pro-rata over every active target's weight and each share capped at what that night's drift row says the target is short, so nothing is bought that the next check would sell; what the caps and the excluded targets leave stays cash.
- Cash drag counts tranches still scheduled and deposits held as cash as intended, so a staging on schedule or a deliberate hold is not flagged.
- An allocation proposal approved in shadow and never placed is expired once its own expiry passes, audited, so the planner is not kept aside on that instrument forever.
- Allocation proposals stay in shadow, refused at approve with shadow mode off, until verify items 60 (plain LIMIT accepted) and 64 (the 17:10 price is not an after-hours print) are confirmed and `Allocation::ORDERS_VERIFIED` is flipped in a deliberate commit.
- A halt waits without counting against a deposit's five deferral nights; a deposit or last tranche given up as cash alerts by email and push; a blocked deployment buy's tripped halt is engaged after its rollback; a deposit with no targets defers and gives up rather than pausing buys for ever.
- Deferred, not built: cancelling waiting allocation proposals when a target is retired (they stay approvable and say which target in their Drift panel); a dashboard action to stop a staging early; a nag on an unanswered deposit question; a cache-only price chart for pages (the thesis review page, like the proposal page since Phase 2, can fetch history on a cold cache).
- **PRD §5.12 is superseded by these answers on four points:** one versioned band, not per target; diversifier and leveraged tags audited but not versioned; a deterministic planner rather than the agent proposing; and a thesis review rather than a "thesis-review proposal".

**Answered 2026-10-16, all as drafted:** rebalance buys are plain LIMIT orders with no bracket stop; rebalance notional counts toward the daily notional cap; one versioned `rebalance_band` rule, no per-target override; `allocation` origin, hand-entered fund flows, tags and authored asset class, and the drift job in shadow all accepted.

The questions as asked:

Rebalance buys as plain LIMIT orders without a bracket stop; `allocation` as a proposal origin; whether rebalance notional counts toward the daily notional cap; fund flows entered by hand until a source is verified; the leveraged and amplified flags as tags and the asset class as an authored column rather than data from a source; no per-target band override (the band is one versioned rule; a per-target band would need versioning of its own); the drift job running in the same shadow mode as everything else, so a shadowed rebalance is placed by hand like any other.
